A leader of a successful ministry is as susceptible to derailment as someone who is under the stress of a failing ministry.

process of assessing the current situation and comparing it to the
original mission goals—then making whatever changes are necessary to
realign with the original mission objectives. It is the continual realigning
of every part of the leader’s life to the mission objective—not just what
you are doing, but who you are.

Congregations expect pastors to lead, but these men and women face the same challenges that every other follower of Christ faces. Although people expect them to assist others in personal transformation, pastors often have no place to turn for the encouragement and coaching required for their own spiritual formation.


We believe that transformation is a process that involves the whole person, and we believe there are three dynamic processes in the Christian life that help to grow the whole person to maturity.

If we could help ministers understand the simple truth that vision is connected to culture not to programs, I think we would eliminate a great deal of burnout.

“Drew is a friend and colleague who has the characteristics of a good coach, and he has specific experience with facilitating peer learning. When he was serving as pastor of a church in a city in the Midwest, he convened a group of six pastors in his city. Meeting monthly for a couple of hours, the group read A Failure of Nerve by Edwin Friedman and then Crucial Conversations by Kerry Patterson, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan, and Al Switzler. They were reading for more than information. They would read a chapter, come together to discuss it, and then make commitments to one another about how they were going to practice what they had learned. When they gathered the next month, they shared the outcome of their practice, discussed another chapter, and went back to their local congregations to practice some more.
