I am not a great prayer. But I go to work with the attitude that says, _God, you’re sovereign. You’ve given me a mind and I want you to use it today. if I start to do anything against what you want, stop me just like that_ [snaps his fingers]. I simply acknowledge my relationship to him.

  

- **Posture of humility:** The writer confesses not being “a great prayer”, which frames prayer not as a performance but as an honest, imperfect relationship.

- **Prayer as daily orientation:** Prayer is embedded into going to work, turning ordinary activity into a context for dependence on God rather than something separate or overly formal.

- **Trust in God’s sovereignty and guidance:** The attitude “God, you’re sovereign… you’ve given me a mind” holds together responsibility (using one’s mind) with reliance (asking God to guide and correct).

- **Openness to correction:** “If I start to do anything against what you want, stop me just like that” expresses a readiness to be interrupted or redirected, not just blessed in whatever one has already decided.

- **Relational, not mechanical:** “I simply acknowledge my relationship to him” captures prayer as awareness and acknowledgement of God’s presence, not just requests or rituals.
