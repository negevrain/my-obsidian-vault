The rhythms and actions of Jesus: the way to success and sustainability in life and leadership.

Obedience - what do I say here… Jesus came to earth and made his home among us. It’s a beautiful picture of the incarnation. Talk about the absolute commitment of Jesus to only doing the will of his father in heaven… that he refuses to do anything else other than that.

How does this relate to leaders today…?

This is extraordinarily difficult. We are after all given by God free will. But whereas Jesus to do the will of his father, our capacity to choose is undermined, tainted by our sin nature…

Solitude & Prayer (Luke 5:16; Luke 6:12) - Jesus would often withdraw - go to a quiet place on his own. Rarely, if ever, however, did Jesus have these times of solitude without praying. The two- solitude and prayer - go hand in hand. Of course, we call it praying, but for Jesus, these were times that he spent with his dad - deep intimacy.

Why: Jesus was God incarnate — which means he was a man, the God man. Which meant that he experienced tiredness, wariness of body and mind and soul… Jesus did this so frequently that’s it quite obvious that his earthly mission …. For him, solitude and prayer was key to his sustainability- he knew that he could not be as productive in fulfilling the mission his father gave him without it.

When: it curious to note that he often went off by himself after times of significant missionary activity, which, just as curiously, preceded more time of intense missionary activity (examples).

It’s also notable that Jesus needed these times of solitude and prayer before making major decisions (e.g., the calling of the twelve disciples).[[]]
