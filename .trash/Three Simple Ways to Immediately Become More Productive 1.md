The [CEO](http://www.inc.com/laura-montini/infographic/the-ceo-bookshelf.html) of luxury watchmaker TAG Heuer said that Apple had made some “fundamental mistakes” designing the Apple watch. Jean-Claude Biver later changed his mind and came out in support of the Apple watch, saying that he would wear one. In doing so Biver missed the opportunity to position his company’s traditional watches as a simple way to increase your [productivity](https://www.google.com/url?q=http://www.inc.com/jeff-haden/10-simple-steps-to-exceptional-daily-productivity.html&sa=U&ei=4HFfVcSTAcGjgwTv84CoDQ&ved=0CAcQFjAB&client=internal-uds-cse&usg=AFQjCNHgo44AzIp3H5JzRWYz_UOOV_rAaw).

Even if you buy one of the 45 million wearables that will be sold by the end of this year, you may want to consider keeping it in the box if you want to drastically [[10harshtruthsthatwi]] your productivity. The true secret to improved productivity involves stepping back in time and detaching yourself from your smartphone, smartwatch, and any other device that beeps, buzzes, or tempts you into the [distraction](http://www.inc.com/magazine/201405/brian-dumaine/how-leaders-focus-with-distractions.html) zone. I have experimented with this over the last 30 days and want to share some ways that may work for you too.

1. **Start wearing a watch again.**

I have an exquisite Tag Heuer watch that I bought 11 years ago in Dubai on the way to our honeymoon in the Maldives. But I had stopped wearing it because I always have my phone so close by, and that told the time for me. Except, of course, I rarely used my phone just to check the time; I might also look at a news site, my email, or twitter, leading to distraction. It was a delight to start wearing my watch again–though it took some time before I remembered to actually look at it! Now that I am relying on my watch again, I’ve found that my phone stays in my purse or pocket a lot longer, and I am more [[10smarttipstopreven]].

1. **Dig out your alarm clock.**

Alarm clock sales are at an all-time low because many people now use the alarm on their phone for their morning wake-up call. Duracell batteries has suffered a 4 percent drop in sales that they attribute in part to a decline in digital radio alarms, and Proctor & Gamble has plans to spin off that business. Keeping your phone by your bed creates a lot of consequences for you personally: it can lead to many distractions just at the point when you need to relax, and it can actually prevent you from falling asleep. Once you wake, the phone at your bedside means you probably start your day by looking at email, tweets, Facebook posts, and other messages. Could you leave your phone in another room or at least across the other side of your bedroom? Try putting your favorite book on your nightstand for a more gentle end to your day so you can wake up refreshed and [energized](http://www.inc.com/christina-desmarais/how-to-use-positive-energy-to-succeed.html).

1. **Bring out your camera.**

The final way that you can keep your phone in your pocket or purse is to start carrying around your camera again. I dusted off my camera from the back of the cupboard, and I have been enjoying taking endless photos without getting distracted by other things. A [study](http://www.washington.edu/news/2015/05/18/study-44-percent-of-parents-struggle-to-limit-cell-phone-use-at-playgrounds/) by the University of Washington of caregivers at playgrounds revealed that 44 percent of caregivers felt that they should minimize the use of cell phones while watching children play, yet they felt guilty that they failed to live up to that standard. Using a camera rather than a cell phone for taking pictures could help with that! Even when out at a restaurant, it is far more acceptable to have your camera out to take pictures than to use your phone. When you use your phone, your companions may think you are also checking email or Facebook at the same time!

In _[Power of Habit](http://www.inc.com/jeff-haden/change-any-habit-painlessly-6-tips.html)_, Charles Duhigg advises that to break old habits you first have to understand your triggers and then create new cues to form new positive habits. Finally he recommends an appropriate reward to reinforce new habits. As I experimented with detaching from my phone, I realized there were a number of habits that I needed to break, and that my cues could be my watch, my alarm clock, and my camera.

I began by leaving my phone on my desk in my home office at the end of my day, not checking it again until the next morning. Then also leaving it on the other side of my office when I am working rather than in reach. As a result, I can focus faster when I am writing, and I am reading more each evening. I also have a deliberate start to my day because the alarm clock is waking me, so I don’t go straight to my phone. The benefit is that I now dedicate more time to my [[whatfailingatthecros]] and [[how30days]] practice. An added benefit is that I am less distracted when with my family.

By starting to reuse these three devices–the watch, the alarm clock, and the camera–you can form some new habits too in order to free up your time and energy from the continuous distractions of your phone. The benefit of greater productivity, both at home and at work, is yours for the taking.
