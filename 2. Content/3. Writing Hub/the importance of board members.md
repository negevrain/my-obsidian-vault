Today I met with a client in Brisbane recently. After the obligatory small talk and niceties, I asked him about the state of his relationship with his board. This has been one of the key focus areas of our coaching conversations for the past year or so.

This time, board issues must have been weighing more heavily on his heart and mind than normal, because almost without meaning to, he blurted out a situation that happened quite recently involving members of his board which has forced my client to take quite drastic action.

The story he told me triggered the start of a very difficult, but important conversation about the role that boards play for organisations like his, and like yours. It doesn’t matter what sort of organisation we’re talking about … it could be a church, or a charity, a social enterprise or a club … whatever it is, boards are critical to the success of an organisation.

The problem is that too many boards, it seems, have members who are ill-equipped to lead and to provide good governance for the organisations that we are trying to lead. As far as my client is concerned, this goes beyond just poor governance and a dysfunctional board. The situation he finds himself in actually borders on bullying at best and criminal at worst.
