**Pastors and nonprofit leaders have always taken a lot of heat when asking for financial support.** After all, projects of any size cost money, whether it’s providing clean water in India, supporting orphans in Eastern Europe, helping the homeless, building a museum, or preaching the gospel anywhere – whatever ministry you feel called to accomplish is going to need money.

Plus, getting the message out about your work via social media, websites, radio, video, or television, costs serious money as well.

Churches, ministry organizations and other nonprofits aren’t profit centers and rely on the generosity of donors. But what’s the best way to ask for financial support – especially with the legion of critics out there waiting to pounce?

I’m no fundraising expert, but over my career, I’ve produced programming for many of the largest media ministries in America, as well as Public Broadcasting and various product infomercials. I’ve produced countless TV commercials and even Super Bowl spots.

So I know at least a thing or two about successfully asking for financial support.

**And here’s my suggestion:**

***Don’t ask for money, invite people to partner in your vision.***

Remember that this is the most marketed to, sold, and pitched generation in history. They can smell a “con” a mile away, and are hyper sensitive to anything that doesn’t seem sincere and authentic. So the minute you ask people for money, red flags go up.

**On the other hand, people want to be part of a bigger vision.** They want to change the world. They want to feel like a partner in something greater than themselves.

**So instead of asking for money, *invite them to be part of your vision*.** Thank them for every donation, keep them updated on the project, and make your appeal about *them*, and not just your *project*.

**And perhaps most important: *show them stories of people whose lives have been transformed because of your work.***

The Bible promises great things to people who are generous. But generosity isn’t just about money, it’s about partnership.

*Joining together to change the world.*

**So start there. It’s less stressful, you’ll be far more natural, and it’s less offensive to your audience. And once they’re onboard with the vision, chances are, the doors will start opening.**
