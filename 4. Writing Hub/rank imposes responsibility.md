
The son of a friend is being bullied at work. He has autism, which makes it harder for him to process what he is experiencing. He finds it difficult to tell the difference between playful banter and malicious put downs.

It turns out the primary culprit is an employee who covers for the business owner when he’s away. According to my friend, our culprit acts like a boss when the real boss is absent. It’s understandable, then, that my friend’s son tries to avoid work when said culprit is put in charge.

Peter Drucker once wrote, “Rank does not confer privilege or give power. It imposes responsibility."

But, responsibility for what…?

Of course it wouldn’t take long to write a list of responsibilities - financial performance, growth, 

But according to Jesus, one responsibility stands out above the rest. For those with rank, those in leadership positions, it is to look after those that have been given to us.

Jesus first modelled this for us, before saying this to His father in Gethsemane, “During my time here, I protected them by the power of the name you gave me. I guarded them so that not one was lost, except the one headed for destruction, as the Scriptures foretold.” — John‬ ‭17‬:‭12‬ ‭NLT‬‬

Jesus then expressed his expectations of those with rank when he said to Peter on the beach, “feed my sheep.”

Leadership has many definitions…

It is servanthood

It is equipping

It is shepherding

It looks like Jesus

ONE thing that leadership does NOT look… is throwing your weight around, acting like the boss.

In light of the example Jesus have us, it’s sobering to think that churches and Christian organisations are fertile breeding grounds for the type of behaviour Jesus actually railed against.

Which raises another question…

How do we train those with rank to lead like Jesus?
