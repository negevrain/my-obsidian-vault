

I’m wrestling with Psalm 127:1 … again.

It says, “Unless the Lord builds a house, the work of the builders is wasted. Unless the Lord protects a city, guarding it with sentries will do no good.”
‭‭
I’ve wrestled with this verse for as long as I can remember, especially in my former life as a leader of a Christian ministry. Each time I read it, I was challenged to confront whether the thing I was leading at the time was actually a house that the Lord wanted to build. And if it was a house the Lord wanted to build, then was I actually allowing him to build the darn thing.

Fast forward to now, and I’m asking myself the same questions… is my coaching business a house that the Lord wants to build (sometimes, I’m not sure) and if it is (hopefully), then am I actually letting him build it.

The wrestle happens because it’s far too easy for me to take over. It’s as though I know better than God (who happens to be THE master builder) how to build a business, grow an organisation, lead a team.

The wrestle also happens because I’ve always needed to know the “how”. How will God build the house called Dare Greatly Coaching?

But at the heart of it all is an disconcerting sense of uncertainty. If God builds the house, then where does that leave me? Where do I fit in? Does he even need me?

I wonder if other Christian leaders and former leaders - pastors, CEOs, ministry leaders - have the same wrestle.

Or am I the only one?

Are you trying to build a church or a business or a not-for-profit because you don’t know how to get out of God’s way? And do you wonder what your role would actually look like if God did exactly that - built the house that is the organisation, the department, the team you’re supposed to be leading?

One of the reasons why I love coaching is that the client and I benefit from the process. If done well, both of us learn and grow in an atmosphere of mutual support and encouragement. In fact, when I work with leaders, either as coach or growth partner, I sometimes wonder if they get as much out of the relationship as I do.

And one of the things I’ve learned as a coach is the importance to partnering with Holy Spirit. God knows the end from the beginning. He knows what the house will look like when it’s complete. Our role is to put ourselves in a position to receive our instructions.
