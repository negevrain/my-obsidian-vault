Be Still


So let me tell you about my morning.

I had a plane to catch. And to get to the airport on time, there’s a bit maths involved. It takes around 90 minutes from home to the long stay parking service, 5-10 minutes to check my car in, another 10 minutes for the shuttle to get me to the domestic terminal, then maybe 10-15 minutes to go through security. And if I’ve timed it right, I’ll have about 30 minutes before boarding the plane.

So I left home thinking I had plenty of time.

What I didn’t factor in was the weather - it bucketed down for a fair chunk of the drive, which caused several accidents ahead of me, which slowed traffic DRAMATICALLY, which forced Google maps to take me on some weird detour. Oh, and did I mention that the drive to the airport was during peak hour in Brisbane.

How stupid am I…?

Then when I get to airport, I’m directed to a special lane in security staffed by overly-enthusiastic guards who insisted on putting by carry-on luggage through their scanner 3 times.

I still don’t know what they were looking for.

When I finally boarded the plane, I had 8 minutes to spare. Only to then be told that the plane wouldn’t be taking off for at least 30 minutes due to … mumble mumble mumble.

I kinda zoned out when they started making the announcement.

But at least I had my 30 minutes to spare.

Anyways, while I was driving, a song came on - Be Still by Steven Curtis Chapman. It’s based on the scripture in [Bible reference] that says, “be still and know that I am God.”

As I listened to the song while stuck in traffic and in very real danger of missing my flight, I realised that God’s invocation isn’t just about finding a quiet place to spend time with Him. It’s just as much about being still in our spirits during times of chaos, turmoil, stupid drivers and hyper-diligent security officers. It’s about knowing that God is in control of our circumstances, even though it sure as hell doesn’t seem like it.

But, as it always is with God, we have a choice to make.

You see, another way of reading that scripture might be: if you choose to be still, to turn off the noise in your heart and mind, then you’ll have a better chance of knowing and seeing that I am God who is sovereign and in control of all your circumstances.

I must confess that I wasn’t all that successful at being still this morning. It got very loud in my head and heart at times. Still, there were moments, few though they were, when I was able to turn the volume down long enough to hear the still small voice of God whisper, “It’s okay. I got this.”
